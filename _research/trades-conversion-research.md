# What Converts Trades Owners on a B2B Marketing-Agency Site (2025–2026)

Research compiled 2026-09-11 for WeConvert (Northern Colorado web design for trades; $800 one-time build + $30/mo, no contract).

**Who we're writing for:** an owner-operator painter/HVAC/roofer/plumber/electrician. Usually 1–15 employees. Reads the site on a phone, often between jobs or at 7pm in the truck. Has almost certainly been burned by an "SEO guy" or a lead-gen company before. Distrusts marketing vocabulary. Buys on: can I understand it, can I see it, can I get out of it, and does the guy sound like he'll actually pick up.

**Important caveat on sourcing:** Reddit and ContractorTalk are blocked to automated fetching from this environment (Reddit is policy-blocked; ContractorTalk returns HTTP 402 behind a paywall proxy). Forum sentiment below is reconstructed from search-result snippets that quote those threads, plus review-platform complaints and agency-side writeups summarizing client objections. Treat the forum voice as directionally accurate, not verbatim-verified. Everything with a number attached has a linked source.

## 1. MESSAGING

### Outcome-led beats service-led — but only when the outcome is concrete

Benefit/outcome-led headlines beat feature/service-led headlines by roughly **27% on average** in landing-page testing, and headlines are the single highest-leverage element to test (winners 24% of the time vs. ~13% for tests generally).
Source: https://www.flint.com/articles/landing-page-headline-testing-statistics

But there's a trap. "More booked jobs" is an outcome; it's also what every one of the 40 agencies that cold-called this owner last year said. The differentiator isn't outcome-vs-service — it's **outcome + mechanism + proof of specificity**. Hook Agency's framework for home-service sites (which is B2C, but the pattern transfers because trades owners judge your site the way they've been taught to judge their own) puts the winning hero formula at **service + location + trust signal**, e.g. "Same-day AC repair you can trust" — not a mission statement.
Source: https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-websites-that-convert/

For a B2B agency selling to that owner, the analogous formula is **outcome + who it's for + geography + constraint**:
- "A website that turns your Google listing into booked estimates. Built for Northern Colorado trades in about a week. $800. No contract."

That does four jobs at once: names the result, qualifies the reader, kills the "long build times" objection, and kills the "locked in" objection — above the fold.

### How specific should the numbers be

Very specific, and only if they're true and small. This audience penalizes round, big, unsourced numbers harder than almost any other buyer. "Grow your revenue 300%" reads as a lie to someone who's seen a lead-gen invoice. "$800. Live in 7 days. You own the domain." reads as a real person.

If you don't have client results yet, **do not invent category stats as if they're your results.** Two honest moves instead:
1. Cite the industry number and attribute it: "Home-service sites average a 7.8% conversion rate. The vague ones run 3–7%. The clear ones run 12–16%." (Source: Hook Agency, link above.) That positions the problem without claiming credit.
2. Use process specificity instead of result specificity: number of days, number of revisions, number of pages, who answers the phone.

### The vocabulary gap — their words vs. the jargon they distrust

**Words they use themselves:**
booked jobs · estimates · bids · quotes · calls · the phone ringing · jobs on the board · a full schedule · leads that answer · my Google listing · showing up on the map · my guys · the truck · slow season · busy season · word of mouth · repeat customers · referrals · what's this gonna run me

**Words that trigger the shields up:**
"leverage" · "synergy" · "omnichannel" · "digital transformation" · "scale your business" · "growth partner" · "we're not an agency, we're a partner" · "10x" · "funnel" (they'll tolerate it, they don't love it) · "solutions" · "unlock" · "empower" · "cutting-edge" · "revolutionary" · "proprietary platform" (this one is actively alarming — see ownership objection) · "AI-powered" (see §7).

Buyers now report that unverified "AI-powered" claims rank in the **top three reasons they abandon a vendor evaluation**, behind only pricing opacity and poor integration support.
Source: https://searchengineland.com/ai-squeezing-marketing-agencies-472189

### The seven objections, ranked by how often they killed a deal

Compiled from agency-side red-flag guides and contractor complaint threads:

1. **"I'll get locked into a contract."** The dominant fear. Agencies use no-contract as a sales hook then bury it in setup fees, long cancellation windows, or asset ownership limits. Owners have learned to ask "what happens if I cancel."
   Sources: https://clicksgeek.com/marketing-agency-with-no-contracts-2026/ · https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/marketing-agency-red-flags

2. **"They'll take my money and go dark."** Reddit users describe long-term agency contracts as *hostage situations* where agencies go complacent after securing guaranteed revenue. Contractors on ContractorTalk reported paying **$500–$1,000/month to SEO companies that had zero clients ranking on page one.**
   Sources: https://www.saashero.net/competitor/lead-generation-agency-reviews-reddit/ · https://www.contractortalk.com/threads/seo-worth-it-recommendation.89576/

3. **"I won't own my own site."** The nightmare story that circulates in this industry: agency hosts on a "proprietary platform," charges to export, puts accounts in its own name. Some HVAC owners literally **couldn't access their own Google Business Profile** because the agency "managed" it.
   Source: https://slash-media.com/blog/questions-to-ask-marketing-agency-before-signing

4. **"I'll pay for leads that don't answer."** One HVAC company reported that of 20+ purchased leads, **only one person admitted they'd actually submitted a request**, and leads were arriving outside their service area. Another set of buyers reported "crappy leads" plus inability to cancel.
   Source: https://www.saashero.net/competitor/lead-generation-agency-reviews-reddit/ · https://www.trustpilot.com/review/contractorlaunch.io

5. **"It'll take six months and cost ten grand."** Standard custom agency builds run **$6,000–$25,000+**; freelancer/small studio $1,500–$6,000. Timeline expectations are measured in months.
   Source: https://www.routeless.io/blog/contractor-website-cost

6. **"I'll get upsold every month."** Red flags owners are coached to watch for: refusing to itemize the retainer, bundling ad spend into the retainer so you can't see it, bait-and-switch staffing (senior partner sells, junior executes).
   Sources: https://slash-media.com/blog/questions-to-ask-marketing-agency-before-signing · https://www.saashero.net/competitor/lead-generation-agency-reviews-reddit/

7. **"They don't understand my trade."** Generalists "spend the first six months learning HVAC on your dime, write blog posts homeowners don't search for, and run ad campaigns built for SaaS funnels."
   Source: https://builtrightdigital.com/best-hvac-marketing-agencies/

**Every one of these is a copy opportunity, because WeConvert's actual offer beats all seven.** $800 flat, no contract, ~1 week, client owns the domain and the site, no lead resale, local. That's not a positioning exercise — the offer is literally the objection-handling. The site's job is to say so before they can raise it.

## 2. TRUST SIGNALS — ranked for this specific audience

### Tier 1 — moves the needle most

**Real photos of real people and real work.** Landing pages with authentic business imagery convert **~35% higher** than stock; replacing stock with real team photos lifts form completions **~29%**; CTAs paired with authentic local imagery see **~47% higher CTR**. 98% of consumers say authentic images matter for trust, and ~90% want transparency around AI-generated imagery. Notably, **slightly imperfect photos often outperform professionally staged ones** — natural light, work clothes, real job sites.
Sources: https://designweblocal.com/local-vs-stock-photography-the-data-behind-authentic-visual-content/ · https://cxl.com/blog/stock-photography-vs-real-photos-cant-use/

Direct implication for WeConvert: a real photo of Danny — one person, named, face visible, in Fort Collins — beats any logo, badge, or illustration on the page. The single biggest fear is "who am I actually going to be able to reach." A face and a phone number answer that.

**Before/after of an actual site you built.** This is the strongest possible proof for a web-design offer because it's self-demonstrating and requires no trust to evaluate. Trades owners are visual and comparison-driven — they sell before/afters themselves. Show the old site (slow, dated, no mobile) next to the new one, with the business name and town.

**Named local clients with the town attached.** "4 J's Painting — Longmont" outperforms "a painting company in Colorado" by a wide margin. Anonymized testimonials read as fabricated to this audience.

**AI-generated imagery is a liability here.** ~90% of consumers want disclosure on AI imagery (source above), and this audience is the most likely to spot and resent it. Use real photos or plain type; do not use AI-generated "happy contractor" hero images.

### Tier 2 — matters, cheap to add

- **Google review screenshots** of the client's own listing, showing the count going up. This is the format they already trust because it's the one they check on competitors.
- **Geographic specificity beyond "Colorado."** Name Fort Collins, Greeley, Loveland, Windsor, Timnath, Wellington by name. Naming the specific town is the single cheapest trust move available and it's also how local search works.
- **A live, clickable demo.** WeConvert's entire cold-call model is "I already built you one." The site should mirror that: a real URL they can open on their phone, not a screenshot.
- **Explicit ownership language.** "The site is yours. The domain is in your name. If you leave, you take it." Directly answers objection #3, which almost no competitor addresses in copy.
- **No-contract stated as a number, not an adjective.** "Cancel any month, 0-day notice" > "flexible terms."

### Tier 3 — weak or actively counterproductive for this audience

- Award badges, "as seen in," agency-partner logos. Reads as marketing-company theater.
- Stock "5 stars" graphics with no source.
- **Any unverified credential.** WeConvert's own hard rule already covers this: never claim licensed/insured/bonded/BBB without confirmation. Also worth noting for the site — a fabricated "10 years in business" or an implied team ("our team of experts") when it's one person is exactly the kind of thing that gets caught and ends the deal.
- Long "About Us" mission narrative above the proof.

## 3. OFFER STRUCTURE — publish the price

The evidence is one-directional here and it's strong.

- **71% of homeowners are more likely to call an HVAC contractor whose website includes pricing.** Trades owners have been told this about their own businesses for two years — so an agency that hides its price is failing its own advice, and they'll notice.
  Source: https://www.achrnews.com/articles/153642-contractors-reap-the-benefits-of-transparent-online-pricing
- **73% of customers prioritize clear upfront pricing** (ServiceTitan, n=1,032 contractors across 7 trades).
  Source: https://www.servicetitan.com/guides/2026-ai-in-the-trades
- Businesses with transparent pricing typically see **23–40% higher conversion rates and shorter sales cycles** than those requiring a price-discovery call.
  Source: https://alexberman.com/what-is-pricing-transparency
- In B2B specifically, across 80 companies and 31M visitors, **62.5% now publish pricing** — hiding it is the minority position and increasingly reads as a tell.
  Source: https://www.hockeystack.com/lab-blog-posts/state-of-pricing-demo-case-study-pages
- Agency-buying guides for contractors explicitly list **"hides pricing until a second call"** as a bad answer and a red flag.
  Source: https://slash-media.com/blog/questions-to-ask-marketing-agency-before-signing

**Verdict: put $800 on the page, above the fold, in the hero.** For this segment, at this price point, the price *is* the value proposition. $800 against a $6,000–$25,000 category benchmark is the most persuasive fact WeConvert owns, and burying it behind "request a quote" throws away the only thing that makes an unknown 20-year-old student outcompete an established agency.

### Framing the $30/mo next to the $800

The market context: the big 2026 structural shift is **from "buy a $10,000 website" to "rent a website for $300/month,"** with trades leading adoption. Typical managed monthly runs **$150–$500/mo**; subscription site plans **$200–$700/mo**.
Source: https://www.routeless.io/blog/contractor-website-cost · https://drivewebdesign.com/contractor-website-cost/

So WeConvert's $30/mo is **5x to 20x below** the market floor. That's a headline-grade fact and it's currently invisible.

**Frame it three ways, in this order:**

1. **Anchor against the category, not against zero.** "$800 once, $30/month after. Most agencies charge $150–$500/month just to keep the lights on." The reader's mental comparison should be to the alternative, not to $0.
2. **Make the $30 concrete and tangible, not a "fee."** Never say "maintenance retainer" or "monthly plan." Say what it buys: *"$30/mo covers hosting, security, backups, and text-change requests. That's it. It's not a marketing retainer."* The word "retainer" is contaminated for this buyer.
3. **Translate it to their unit economics.** A trades owner prices in jobs, not dollars. "$30/month is less than one gallon of premium paint" or, better, "$360/year — one repaint job pays for four years of it." This is the single most persuasive reframe available and it costs one sentence.

Also: state the **total first-year number** explicitly — **$1,160 the first year, $360 after.** Hiding the annualized number is what agencies do, and this buyer will do the math anyway. Doing it for them is a trust signal.

**Do not** offer a contract on the page. WeConvert's rule is contracts only if the client asks. Putting a "3-month term" option on the site reintroduces the #1 objection unprompted.

## 4. PAGE STRUCTURE

### Above the fold — non-negotiable elements

NN/g's scrolling research: users spend **~57% of viewing time above the fold**, with sharp decline after — but ~43% of attention does happen below, and people scroll only if the top content creates "information scent" that there's more worth reading. The fold's two jobs are: state the value proposition, and signal there's more.
Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention-original-research/ · https://cxl.com/blog/above-the-fold/

Hook Agency's home-service fold checklist — confirm the service, confirm the service area, one clear CTA, proof directly underneath, no rotating sliders, no abstract headline. 64% of home-services traffic is mobile; **53% abandon a site that takes 3+ seconds to load**, and each additional second cuts conversions ~32%.
Source: https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-websites-that-convert/

**WeConvert's fold should contain, in order:**
1. H1: outcome + audience + geography
2. Subhead: the price and the terms, verbatim — "$800 one-time. $30/month. No contract. Usually live in a week."
3. Primary CTA button
4. Tap-to-call number, visible without scrolling, on mobile
5. One line of proof or one town-name list — creates the scent to keep scrolling

### First CTA position

**Zero scroll.** Not "one screen down." This audience's session is short and interrupted. The CTA appears in the hero and then repeats after every major section — Hook's guidance is "repetition without pressure," with one primary action and secondary actions kept subtle.

### Recommended section order

1. **Hero** — headline, price, terms, CTA, phone.
2. **The problem, in their words** — one short block: the site is dated/slow/invisible on a phone, so the calls go to the other guy. No statistics dump here; one honest line beats five stats.
3. **Before / after** — a real site you rebuilt. This is the highest-value section on the page and should come before any explanation of process. Show, then tell.
4. **What you get** — a plain bulleted list. Pages, mobile, tap-to-call, Google Business Profile connected, contact form to your email/phone. Concrete nouns only.
5. **Price block** — $800 / $30 with the category anchor and the annual total. Repeat "no contract, you own it."
6. **Objection block** — this is the section almost nobody builds and it's the one that closes this segment. Four to six short "you're probably wondering" items, answered in one sentence each. (See FAQ list below.)
7. **Proof** — named local clients, Google review screenshots, town names.
8. **Who you are** — real photo, real name, real phone, Fort Collins. Short. One paragraph. Honesty about being small is an asset here; owner-to-owner beats agency-to-client.
9. **FAQ** — the longer list.
10. **Final CTA** — restated price, restated terms, phone number large.

### Form length

- HubSpot's analysis of 40,000+ landing pages: **3 fields converts best (~25%+)**, and going from 3 fields to 4 shows a sharp decline. Reducing 4→3 can lift conversions up to ~50%. Each additional field costs ~4.1% on average.
  Source: https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/optimize-conversion-forms · https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-points
- Baymard-sourced friction data: a **required phone number makes ~37% of users hesitate**; company name feels invasive at early stage.
  Source: https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-points

**For WeConvert, use exactly 3 fields: Name · Phone · Business name.** Drop email entirely — this buyer converts by phone, and email is the field that produces the dead leads. Phone is required here despite the 37% friction stat, because the entire fulfillment model is a call; the tradeoff is intentional. Make the third field "Business name" rather than "Message" — it's one tap of autofill and it lets you look them up before calling.

### Phone vs. form vs. calendar — clear hierarchy for this audience

**Phone > form > calendar. Do not put a calendar booking widget as the primary CTA.**

Reasoning, with support:
- Phone calls carry the highest intent; "someone who calls is closer to buying than someone who fills a form and waits."
  Source: https://www.revenue.io/inside-sales-glossary/what-is-click-to-call
- This audience lives on the phone by occupation. Their own customers call them. A calendar link asks them to learn a new interaction pattern and commit to a future timeslot — both are friction for someone who will decide in 90 seconds or not at all.
- The category data on responsiveness is brutal and cuts both ways: average caller patience before hanging up has dropped from 8 seconds (2020) to **2.3 seconds (2026)**, and **78% of callers who hit voicemail call a competitor within two minutes.**
  Source: https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/ai-receptionist-missed-calls-solution · https://calljolt.com/blog/guides/home-service-business-missed-call-statistics

That last stat is an operational warning for WeConvert, not just copy. If the site's primary CTA is a phone number and that number goes to voicemail during school hours, the site is actively burning leads. Either the form is primary during those windows, or the voicemail greeting has to set a specific callback time.

Offer a calendar link only as a **tertiary** option, labeled in their language: "Or pick a time that works" — never "Book a discovery call."

### Mobile

- Phone number sticky or in a fixed bottom bar, `tel:` linked, thumb-reachable.
- Single-column, single-thumb navigation.
- The price must be readable without pinch-zoom.
- Load under 3 seconds — which for WeConvert means watching image weight. Note the existing memory item: demo sites deploy as single files with base64-embedded images, which is correct for rendering but makes page weight the thing to watch. Compress before embedding.

## 5. CTA COPY

### What the test data actually says

- **First-person framing is the most reliably replicated CTA finding there is.** Michael Aagaard's Unbounce test changed "Start **your** free 30 day trial" to "Start **my** free 30 day trial" — one word — and click-through to the payment page rose **90%**. Later replications typically land in the 10–40% range, but the direction is consistent.
  Source: https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2015/02/20/call-to-action-buttons
- **Specific beats generic.** ContentVerve changed "Order Information" to "Get Your Free Quote": **+38% clicks**.
  Source: https://getaiform.com/blog/cta-button-examples-guide-2026
- **Button copy matters far more than button color.** Stop optimizing the color.
  Source: https://getaiform.com/blog/cta-button-examples-guide-2026
- Context flips winners: PartnerStack lifted conversions **111%** going from "Book a Demo" to "Get Started" — in SaaS, where "demo" implies a sales gauntlet. That's the opposite context from ours and shouldn't be copied.
  Source: https://getaiform.com/blog/cta-button-examples-guide-2026
- Hook Agency on home services specifically: descriptive beats vague — "Request Service Today" outperforms "Submit."
  Source: https://hookagency.com/blog/home-service-websites-that-convert/

### Ranked for WeConvert

**Strong — use these:**
- **"See My Demo Site"** — best option available. First-person ("my"), specific, zero-commitment, and it's literally true: a demo already exists. Nothing else on the market can say this.
- **"Show Me What Mine Would Look Like"** — longer, works well as the secondary/mid-page CTA.
- **"Call Danny — (970) XXX-XXXX"** — a named human on a button converts better than "Contact Us" for a distrust-primed buyer. Use the actual number as the label on mobile.
- **"Get My Price"** — works only if the price is *not* already on the page. Since it will be, it's redundant. Skip.

**Weak — avoid:**
- "Get Started" — vague; doesn't say what starts or what it costs. Won a SaaS test, wrong context here.
- "Learn More" — signals reading homework.
- "Submit" — worst performer in the category data.
- "Book a Free Consultation" / "Schedule a Discovery Call" — every agency that cold-called this owner used these words. Instant pattern-match to "sales call."
- "Request a Quote" — fine generally, wrong for WeConvert. It implies the price is hidden, which contradicts the whole strategy.
- "Claim Your Free Audit" — "claim" and "free audit" are the two most burned phrases in contractor-facing marketing.

**Microcopy under the button does real work here.** One line: *"Takes 30 seconds. No contract, no sales pitch — I'll just show you the site."* Directly pre-answers the "this is a trap" reflex.

## 6. WHAT TRADES OWNERS ACTUALLY ASK — 12 real questions

Drawn from contractor-facing agency-vetting guides and forum complaint patterns. These are the questions they ask on the call; putting them on the page moves the close forward by a week.

1. What's this going to cost me — all in, including anything recurring?
2. Am I locked into a contract? What happens if I want out in three months?
3. Who owns the website and the domain if we stop working together? Can I take it with me?
4. How long until it's actually live?
5. Have you done this for anyone in my trade? Can I see it — and can I call them?
6. Do you have clients around here, or are you across the country?
7. How many leads am I going to get? (Trap question — see answer angle below.)
8. Are you selling these same leads to my competitors?
9. Who's actually doing the work — you, or someone overseas?
10. What do you need from me, and how much of my time is this going to take?
11. Can I change stuff myself later, or do I have to pay you every time I want a photo swapped?
12. Will it show up on Google? Do you handle my Google listing?

Secondary questions that come up on second calls:
- What happens if the site goes down at 8pm on a Saturday?
- Are you going to try to sell me ads next month?
- How do I know you'll still be in business in a year? *(For WeConvert specifically, this one will come up. Answer it head-on rather than hoping it doesn't.)*
- Is it going to work on a phone? Most of my customers are on their phone.

Sources: https://slash-media.com/blog/questions-to-ask-marketing-agency-before-signing · https://www.uptocodedigital.com/blog/questions-before-hiring-marketing-agency · https://www.thomastowndigital.com/blog/contractor-marketing-agency-questions-to-ask

## 7. 2025–2026 SPECIFICS

### The "AI-run agency" question — honest answer: liability, not asset, as a headline

This matters because WeConvert's own internal identity is "an AI-run company." **Do not put that on the website.**

The evidence:

- Unverified "AI-powered" claims are now a **top-three reason B2B buyers abandon a vendor evaluation**, behind pricing opacity and poor integration support. "If you're still leading with 'AI-powered' in your positioning, you might be actively losing deals."
  Source: https://searchengineland.com/ai-squeezing-marketing-agencies-472189
- **61% of marketers distrust "AI-powered" labels.** If the people who write the labels don't believe them, the contractor reading them certainly doesn't.
  Source: https://www.influencers-time.com/61-of-marketers-now-distrust-ai-powered-labels-data-shows/
- In the trades specifically: **~25% of contractors use AI meaningfully while nearly 50% actively lack trust in it.** 74% see AI as an efficiency lever in principle, but only ~25% are using it — that gap is the skepticism.
  Sources: https://www.servicetitan.com/guides/2026-ai-in-the-trades · https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ai-adoption-residential-contracting/
- **65.5% of business owners worry that leaning on AI makes their business feel less personal or authentic to customers** — meaning an AI-forward pitch triggers the exact fear ("I won't be able to reach a human") that's already the #1 objection.
  Source: https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-guide-2026
- ~90% of consumers want transparency around AI-generated imagery — so AI-generated hero images are a downside risk with no upside.
  Source: https://designweblocal.com/local-vs-stock-photography-the-data-behind-authentic-visual-content/

**The resolution:** AI is the reason WeConvert can charge $800 instead of $8,000 and deliver in a week instead of two months. Sell the **consequence**, not the mechanism. "$800, live in a week" *is* the AI story, told in a way this buyer believes.

If asked directly on a call: be straightforward — yes, AI tools do a lot of the build, that's why it's $800 and not $8,000, and a human (you, by name) reviews and is responsible for every page. Honesty about it lands fine one-on-one. **On the site, it's a headline that costs deals.** The market guidance agrees: "AI is not the offer by itself — the offer is a more disciplined way to produce a marketing result, with stronger economics, visible controls, and less wasted effort."
Source: https://almcorp.com/blog/how-to-sell-ai-marketing-services-to-skeptical-clients-in-2026-positioning-proof-and-pricing-that-close-deals/

One narrow exception where AI *is* a trust asset with this buyer: **AI answering the phone**, because it solves a pain they feel daily (see below). "AI built your website" reads as corner-cutting; "AI answers your phone at 9pm so you stop losing jobs" reads as a tool. Relevant to the shelved Lead Response Systems line — that's the AI story worth telling when it's active.

### Google Local Services Ads and GBP are now the center of gravity

- **LSA visibility grew from 11% of local queries (early 2025) to 31% (November 2025).** LSAs are increasingly appearing in AI Overviews, voice results, and the local map pack.
  Source: https://www.leadtruffle.co/blog/complete-guide-google-lsa-home-service-contractors-2026/
- Blended home-services LSA cost per lead: **$53** across 888 contractors and $6.72M in spend, **43.9% book rate, $233 cost per paying customer** (Feb 2026 benchmark). LSA conversion 20–25% vs. 6–8% for traditional PPC.
  Source: https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/google-local-service-ads-complete-guide-2026
- **October 2025: Google consolidated all trust badges into a single "Google Verified" checkmark.**
  Source: https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/google-local-service-ads-complete-guide-2026
- Contractors running both LSA and SEO generate **42% more total leads at 40% lower CAC** than single-channel operators.
  Source: https://www.geekpoweredstudios.com/post/seo-vs-google-local-services-ads-contractors-2026
- AI Overviews reward verified, well-reviewed entities — which means GBP completeness and review volume now feed both the map pack *and* the AI answer.

**Copy implication:** "connected to your Google Business Profile" is now a more compelling line than "SEO optimized." It's concrete, it's the thing they already check, and it's where the AI-era visibility actually comes from. Also: the site should be built so the GBP and the site reinforce each other (NAP consistency, service-area pages, review embed), and the copy should say that in plain terms.

**Ownership note tied to this:** since agencies holding GBP hostage is a known horror story, "your Google listing stays in your name — I'll never be the owner on it" is a differentiating line almost no competitor writes.

### The missed-call crisis is the best proof-of-problem available

- **62% of calls to home-service businesses go unanswered** in one dataset; an analysis of 130,175 calls across 45 home-service businesses found **74.1% went completely unanswered.** After-hours pickup is often below 20%.
  Sources: https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/ai-receptionist-missed-calls-solution · https://calljolt.com/blog/guides/home-service-business-missed-call-statistics
- **78% of callers who hit voicemail call a competitor within two minutes.** Caller patience: 2.3 seconds in 2026, down from 8 in 2020.
- After-hours AI answering is reported to increase revenue 15–25% for most contractors; one HVAC client's booking rate went 55% → 90%.
  Source: https://mostailabs.com/ai-for/home-services

# COPY DIRECTIVES

Fifteen specific instructions for writing the WeConvert site.

1. **Put $800 in the hero.** Not on a pricing page, not below the fold — in the subhead, in the first 15 words the visitor reads. The price is the strongest asset the business owns and hiding it forfeits the fight against established agencies. (71% of homeowners prefer contractors who post prices; 73% of trades customers prioritize upfront pricing; transparent pricing correlates with 23–40% higher conversion.)

2. **Use the headline formula: outcome + audience + geography + constraint.** Model: *"A website that gets Northern Colorado trades more booked jobs. $800 one-time. Live in about a week."* Never a mission statement, never a rotating slider, never an abstract headline. Confirm what it is, who it's for, and where — above the fold.

3. **Kill all four objections in the first screen, in this exact order: price, contract, timeline, ownership.** One line: *"$800 one-time. $30/month for hosting. No contract. You own the site and the domain."* Every one of those is a top-7 objection and answering them unprompted is the strongest possible trust move with a burned buyer.

4. **Primary CTA label is "See My Demo Site."** First-person "my" (the single most replicated CTA finding — up to 90% lift), specific, zero-risk, and uniquely true for this business. Secondary: *"Show Me What Mine Would Look Like."* Tertiary (mobile, sticky): the phone number itself as the button label.

5. **Banned CTA labels:** "Get Started," "Learn More," "Submit," "Book a Free Consultation," "Schedule a Discovery Call," "Claim Your Free Audit," "Request a Quote." The last four are the exact phrases every agency that cold-called this owner already used.

6. **Three form fields, max: Name · Phone · Business name.** Drop email. (3 fields is the peak-converting count; 4th field causes a sharp decline; each added field costs ~4.1%.) Put one line of microcopy under the button: *"Takes 30 seconds. No contract, no sales pitch — I'll just show you the site."*

7. **Explain why it's only $800 before they can decide it's too cheap.** At a $6,000–$25,000 category benchmark, an unexplained low price reads as risk. Explain it honestly and structurally: no office, no account manager, no sales team, one person, modern tools. Never say "AI-powered."

8. **Never write "AI-powered," "AI-run," "AI agency," or "AI-built" on the site.** Unverified AI claims are a top-3 reason buyers abandon vendor evaluations; 61% of marketers distrust the label; ~50% of contractors actively distrust AI; 65.5% of owners fear AI makes a business feel less personal. Sell the consequence — the price and the speed — not the mechanism. Answer honestly if asked on a call.

9. **Use no AI-generated images anywhere.** ~90% of consumers want AI-imagery disclosure, and this audience is the most likely to spot it. Real photos beat stock by ~35% on conversion and real team photos lift form completions ~29% — and slightly imperfect, natural-light photos outperform polished staged ones.

10. **Put a real photo of Danny, with a real first name and a real phone number, on the page.** The core fear is "I won't be able to reach anyone." A face and a number answer it. Don't write "our team" or "we" when it's one person — a burned buyer will catch the inflation, and owner-to-owner is an advantage here, not a weakness.

11. **Make before/after the third section, ahead of any process explanation.** Show a real old site next to a real new one, with the business name and town. Self-demonstrating proof beats claims for a visual, comparison-driven buyer and requires zero trust to evaluate.

12. **Name towns, not the state.** Fort Collins, Loveland, Greeley, Windsor, Timnath, Wellington, Berthoud, Johnstown. "Serving Colorado" is a non-signal. Town names are simultaneously the cheapest trust move and a local-SEO asset.

13. **Write the ownership guarantee as a standalone, quotable line:** *"The site is yours and the domain is in your name. Your Google listing stays in your name — I'll never be the owner on it. If you leave, you take everything with you."* Agencies holding GBP and domains hostage is the industry's signature horror story and almost no competitor addresses it in copy.

14. **Translate $30/month into their units, in one sentence.** *"$360 a year — one job pays for four years of it."* And state the first-year total, $1,160, rather than making them do the math. Never call it a "retainer," "plan," or "package." Say exactly what it buys: hosting, security, backups, text changes.

15. **Replace "SEO" with "connected to your Google Business Profile."** LSA/GBP visibility grew from 11% to 31% of local queries in 2025 and now feeds AI Overviews and the map pack. "SEO" is a burned word for contractors who paid $500–$1,000/month to firms with zero page-one clients; GBP is concrete and it's the thing they already check on competitors.

**Operational note that affects the copy:** if the phone number is the primary CTA, it cannot go to voicemail. 78% of callers who hit voicemail call a competitor within two minutes, and caller patience is down to 2.3 seconds. Either the form leads during school hours, or the voicemail greeting names a specific callback window.

# READY-TO-USE FAQ LIST

Eight questions with answer angles. Keep each answer to two or three sentences — short answers read as confident, long answers read as defensive.

**1. "Am I locked into a contract?"**
*Angle:* Lead with the flat no, then over-deliver on proof. No contract, no cancellation window, no notice period — stop any month and nothing gets held. Mention the optional 3-month term only as a thing you'll write up *if the client wants one for their own peace of mind*, never as a default. State it as terms, not adjectives: "cancel any month" beats "flexible."

**2. "Who owns the website and the domain?"**
*Angle:* "You do, from day one." Domain registered in their name with their email as the admin. If they leave, they take the site, the domain, and the content. Explicitly add that their Google Business Profile stays in their name and you are never the owner on it — this is the detail that separates you from the firms that created the horror stories.

**3. "What's it really cost — is there anything else?"**
*Angle:* Full itemization, no asterisks. $800 once, $30/month, $1,160 the first year and $360 every year after. Name what's *not* charged: no setup fee, no hosting fee on top, no per-change fee, no ad spend bundled in. Then the anchor: most agencies charge $150–$500/month just for upkeep.

**4. "How long until it's live?"**
*Angle:* About a week from the day they send photos and confirm the details. Say what you need from them up front — photos of their work, service list, service area, phone number — and roughly how much of their time it costs (under an hour total). Time cost is a real objection for someone who's on a job site all day, and naming it small removes it.

**5. "How many leads will I get?"**
*Angle:* Refuse the guarantee, and make the refusal the selling point. "Anybody who promises you a lead number is guessing or lying — I won't do that." Then redirect to what you actually control: the site loads on a phone, shows the work, and makes calling one tap. Note that guaranteeing lead volume is on every contractor-facing red-flag list, so refusing it reads as credibility, not weakness.

**6. "Are you selling my leads to other contractors?"**
*Angle:* Flat no, with the structural reason. You build the site; the calls and form submissions go directly to their phone and never pass through you. You don't run a lead-gen business and there's no shared pool. This directly answers the most-burned experience in the category (the HVAC owner who bought 20+ leads and found one was real).

**7. "Why is this so much cheaper than everyone else?"**
*Angle:* Answer it structurally and honestly, because an unexplained low price reads as risk. No office, no sales team, no account manager, no project manager — one person using modern tools, working directly with the owner. You're building a reputation in Northern Colorado, not maximizing margin on the first ten clients. Do not say "AI" here.

**8. "Who am I actually going to be dealing with?"**
*Angle:* Name yourself, put the photo nearby, give the direct number. "Me. Same number, same person, before and after you pay." Contrast implicitly with the bait-and-switch pattern (senior partner sells, junior executes) that owners are warned about. If the "will you be around in a year" version comes up, answer it directly: you're local, the site and domain are in their name regardless, and nothing about their business is trapped inside your account.
